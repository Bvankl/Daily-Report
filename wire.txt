-International Events-

North Africa: This morning the Ukrainian War expanded to include Libya, after Ukrainian forces conducted a drone boat attack on Russian vessels off the coast of Benghazi. Multiple Unmanned Surface Vessels (USVs) were utilized by Ukraine to strike a Russian cargo vessel (possibly the M/V *ANGARA*), while other USVs targeted the Russian Corvette MERKURY sitting pierside in Benghazi. The MERKURY was sunk, and the status of the freighter remains unknown.

Analyst Comment: This strike is a big deal, as the Ukrainian War is now officially (and technically) a global conflict. Of course, one strike (while effective) is not going to turn the tide of the war. But the grand question remains...where were the drone boats launched from? If Ukraine has a land-based presence outside of their own borders, this is now an Expeditionary war, which has great potential to continue spilling outside the bounds of eastern Europe.

Analyst Comment: In the Middle East, a brief saga took place in the Persian Gulf, and the heavy fog of war has not made it easy to determine what happened, versus what is *claimed* to have happened. On Saturday night, Iran decided to launch a new tactic, dubbed Operation Fereydoun. This plan revolved around a large number of tanker vessels beginning to maneuver toward the Gulf of Oman and attempting to breach the American blockade line. Within Iranian media, this blockade-run was highly publicized, and the first ship was even named the *KHAT SHEKAN*, which translates to "Line Breaker" in Persian.

As of this morning, it's questionable as to whether or not this plan was actually conducted at all. GPS spoofing is heavy in the region right now, and there is a lot of evidence to suggest that the Iranian ships are not actually attempting to breach the American blockade line, but rather their GPS track is being spoofed to appear that these ships are making a run for it. There is also some evidence to suggest that some of these ships don't actually exist, and are instead fabricated AIS tracks showing up on ship tracking websites. The *KHAT SHEKAN* is almost certainly a fake ship, and her AIS track has bounced around the region all weekend. This afternoon, official Iranian social media pages are denying the entire affair, saying that they never intended to run the blockade after all.

Bottom line, shenanigans are afoot in the Strait of Hormuz, and this time it's not just the Americans playing games on the information front. It is rather interesting that Iran feels the need to conduct a fake operation at this time, when they haven't really needed to in the past. If Iran is genuinely trying to run the gauntlet (either now, or at some point in the immediate future), it's highly likely the US Navy will intercept these vessels just like they have before. However, Iran has probably managed to sneak some tankers through the American blockade line, using a variety of trickery and ship-to-ship transfers of their own. The grand question remains of whether or not these logistical efforts will reignite the war, at a time when diplomatic efforts to cool the situation are not on the table.
